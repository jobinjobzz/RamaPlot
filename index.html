The Science

What is a Ramachandran Plot?

Background & History

The Ramachandran plot was introduced by G.N. Ramachandran, C. Ramakrishnan, and V. Sasisekharan in 1963. It maps the backbone dihedral angles φ (phi) and ψ (psi) of each amino acid residue in a protein, revealing which conformations are sterically allowed.

φ is the dihedral angle around the N–Cα bond, and ψ is around the Cα–C bond. Because of steric clashes between atoms, only certain (φ, ψ) combinations are energetically favourable — these define the known secondary structures: α-helices cluster near (−63°, −43°), β-sheets near (−120°, +130°), and left-handed helices near (+60°, +40°).

A well-refined structure at 2.0 Å resolution should have over 90% of residues in the most favoured regions. It remains one of the primary tools for validating crystal structures deposited in the Protein Data Bank (PDB).

Ramachandran G.N., Ramakrishnan C., Sasisekharan V. (1963). Stereochemistry of polypeptide chain configurations. J. Mol. Biol. 7, 95–99.

Region Definitions

RamaPlot uses density-based region boundaries calibrated to match PROCHECK/MolProbity statistics from the Top500 high-resolution crystal structure dataset (Lovell et al. 2003):

  • Most favoured (A, B, L, p) — core α-helix, β-sheet, left-handed helix, and polyproline II (~22% of φ/ψ space)
  • Additional allowed (a, b, l, p) — sterically acceptable loop conformations (~15%)
  • Generously allowed (~a, ~b, ~l, ~p) — borderline conformations (~10%)
  • Disallowed — sterically unfavourable — flags potential modelling errors

Glycine is plotted separately because its lack of a side chain gives it much greater conformational freedom. Proline and pre-proline residues are also treated separately due to their constrained φ angle (~−60°).

Lovell S.C. et al. (2003). Structure validation by Cα geometry: φ,ψ and Cβ deviation. Proteins 50, 437–450.

Understanding the Plot

What the Regions Mean

Each point on a Ramachandran plot represents one amino acid residue. Its position reveals the backbone conformation and whether it is sterically favourable. Glycine, Proline, and β-branched residues are plotted separately.

  • Most Favoured Regions >90% expected
    Core α-helix (A, φ≈−63°, ψ≈−43°), β-sheet (B, φ≈−120°, ψ≈+130°), left-handed helix (L, φ≈+60°, ψ≈+40°), and polyproline II. A well-refined structure at ≥2.0 Å should have >90% of non-Gly, non-Pro residues here.
  • Additional Allowed a, b, l, p
    Sterically acceptable conformations outside the core regions. Common in loop regions, turns, and irregular secondary structures. No structural error implied.
  • Generously Allowed ~a, ~b, ~l, ~p
    Borderline conformations. Residues here warrant closer inspection in the model, especially if corroborated by unusual B-factors or ERRAT warnings nearby.
  • Disallowed Regions Outliers
    Sterically unfavourable. May indicate modelling errors, active-site residues under strain, crystal packing artefacts, or residues requiring further refinement. Always investigate in the context of electron density maps.

Ramachandran in 1963, it is used to assess stereochemical quality — well-refined structures have over 90% of non-Gly, non-Pro residues in the most favoured regions.
RamaPlot runs five PROCHECK-style analyses: (1) Ramachandran plot with 5 residue-class sub-plots, (2) Chi1-Chi2 side-chain torsion plot with omega angle statistics, (3) ERRAT non-bonded interaction analysis, (4) Bond length and angle Z-scores vs Engh & Huber ideal values, and (5) Clash score and B-factor distribution. Outliers are residues whose φ/ψ angles fall outside the generously allowed regions as defined by calibrated PROCHECK boundaries (based on the Top500 high-resolution dataset).
